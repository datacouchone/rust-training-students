DAY 05 / 09

A byte stream has no message boundaries

TCP delivers ordered bytes, not one read per application message.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Decode explicit lengths and preserve message boundaries independently of read boundaries.

WHY THIS MATTERS

What must survive if the caller cancels after the first six bytes?

DAY 05 / 09

Cancellation can discard local progress

Cancellation destroys the future while the stream has already consumed part of the header.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Move progress from temporary locals into the decoder owner.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Why can repeating this function misinterpret body bytes as a new header?

DAY 05 / 09

Persist progress in the decoder

Keeping the header is insufficient if cancellation can also interrupt the body.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Retain header progress, expected length and body progress as one decoder state.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Where must the progress update sit relative to the next await?

DAY 05 / 09

EOF, empty frame and truncation

Empty payload, clean end of stream and a truncated frame have different meanings.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Report Some(empty), None and UnexpectedEof separately.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Why is an empty Vec not the same as None here?

DAY 05 / 09

Length limits and terminal stream errors

An invalid length can trigger unbounded allocation or leave the reader out of sync.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Validate length before resizing and mark terminal protocol failure.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Why is “skip the bad header and try again” unsafe as a protocol policy?

DAY 05 / 09

Regression at every byte boundary

One carefully chosen split cannot cover every cancellation boundary.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Sweep every byte split and compare the reconstructed message.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Why is a single cancellation point insufficient?

Prepare, then commit

Cardinality is a resource boundary

A bound on one record does not bound the number of tenant records.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Check new-tenant cardinality before allocating an entry.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Why is checking accounts.len() >= limit alone too strict?

DAY 05 / 09

Structured events need correlation

Events from concurrent requests interleave, making a plain log sequence misleading.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Carry request identity, tenant, attempt and outcome through structured events.

WHY THIS MATTERS

What can the default instrument behavior accidentally record?

DAY 05 / 09

Deadlock versus slow work

Slow completion and deadlock can look identical in one timeout log.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Look for a dependency cycle and evidence of progress over time.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Does switching a blocking mutex to an async mutex remove the cycle?

DAY 05 / 09

Task leakage after each request

A completed response can leave a task that no request still owns.

WHAT WE CHANGE

Track spawned work through cancellation and observed termination.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Why can request success metrics miss this failure?
